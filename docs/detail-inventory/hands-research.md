# Research: anime/chibi hand drawing conventions for a flat-SVG chibi figure

Scope: relaxed hanging hand (front view) and hand gripping a vertical pole
(staff/hilt), at "mitten with thumb bump" starting point, target size ~180px
figure (head r ~21px) up to 3x that.

## 1. Levels of detail

- Chibi/SD hands commonly drop to 3-4 fingers, or even omit fingers entirely,
  keeping only a thumb bump; three named options are "all 5 fingers," "just a
  thumb," or "no fingers at all," left to the artist's preference.
  Source: https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4868 (also summarized via search, see also https://www.diy.org/blogs/how-to-draw-anime-hands)
- A named simplified chibi hand style: divide the hand into exactly two
  shapes, the thumb and a single four-finger mass (i.e. mitten + thumb, no
  finger separation at all). Source: https://myotakuworld.com/draw-anime-hands/ (via search summary) and https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-and-manga-hands/
- Even full/standard anime hands are commonly simplified for anything but a
  close-up: background or distant characters skip fingernails and most
  interior creasing, keeping just the outline of the major hand masses.
  Source: https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-and-manga-hands/
- General "mitten first" construction method used across skill levels: block
  in the whole hand (or the four fingers together) as one mitten/glove
  silhouette, then decide how much finger separation to cut into it,
  depending on distance/detail level. Sources: https://www.animeoutline.com/draw-anime-hands-holding-something-step-by-step/ , https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4890
- Implied ladder of detail (synthesized from the above, not stated as a single
  list by one source): (1) plain mitten silhouette, no thumb separated; (2)
  mitten + thumb bump (current baseline); (3) + one index-finger separation
  line and/or a knuckle-crease wave near the base of the fingers; (4) + finger
  joint creases (2nd/3rd joint lines) without full separation; (5) fully
  separated finger silhouettes with 3-segment tapering and creases. [This
  5-step ladder itself is my own synthesis/inference, not a source's explicit
  list.]

## 2. Proportions

- Hand length (wrist to fingertip) is roughly comparable to face height
  (chin-to-hairline) in standard anime proportions; chibi shrinks this to
  about 1/2 to 1/3 of that same reference for a cuter, smaller-hand look.
  Source: https://jerwoodvisualarts.org/blog/how-to-draw-anime-hands/
- Within the hand: the middle finger is close in length to the palm itself
  (roughly a 1:1 palm-to-middle-finger ratio). Source: https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-and-manga-hands/
- Finger length hierarchy: middle finger longest and thickest; index and ring
  fingers a bit shorter and thinner than the middle; pinky shortest and
  thinnest of all. Source: https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-and-manga-hands/
- Each finger's 3 segments are uneven: the base-to-first-joint segment is the
  longest, and the section from the last joint to the tip is the shortest.
  Source: https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-and-manga-hands/
- Thumb: comes off the palm at roughly a 45-degree angle from the base of the
  palm (not beside the index finger), and its tip, at that 45-degree angle,
  lands approximately at the index finger's base knuckle, not reaching as far
  as the index finger's second joint. Sources: https://www.diy.org/blogs/how-to-draw-anime-hands (45° wedge off the palm), https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-and-manga-hands/ (tip position)
- Basic block-in shapes given by one guide: palm as a squarish, almost-square
  rectangle; each finger as a tapered 3-segment cylinder; thumb as a wedge at
  ~45°. Source: https://www.diy.org/blogs/how-to-draw-anime-hands
- Chibi construction shorthand: palm as a bread-loaf-like shape with a slight
  arc at the knuckle line, thumb placed via an added circle/bump at the
  palm's side. Source: https://myotakuworld.com/draw-anime-hands/ (via search summary)

## 3. Relaxed hanging hand, front view

- Construction sequence for a hand relaxed at a character's side: start with
  a rounded-corner square for the palm's side profile, then add a second,
  smaller rounded rectangle overlapping it to seat the thumb's base.
  Source: (AnimeOutline family tutorial) surfaced via search; see https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-and-manga-hands/
- Thumb placement is called out as the detail that most determines whether
  the relaxed hand reads as convincing, more than finger detail.
  Source: search summary of https://gvaat.com/blog/how-to-draw-anime-hands-a-step-by-step-tutorial/ and related AnimeOutline material
- A relaxed hand's outline should read with soft, curved gesture lines rather
  than straight/angular ones, fingers slightly curved rather than straight,
  each tapering toward the tip. Source: https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-and-manga-hands/
- No source found gives a named minimal-line spec exclusively for a
  side-hanging front-view hand (palm-to-thigh) at chibi scale beyond the
  general points above. The read-as-a-hand minimum (single index-separation
  line, one thumb crease, one or two knuckle bumps on the outline) is my own
  inference from the general "mitten + selective separation" principle in
  section 1, not stated as a specific pose recipe by any source found.

## 4. Gripping hand around a vertical pole/hilt

- Construction order: block the object in only after establishing the hand's
  overall proportions, then carve individual fingers out afterward, i.e.
  hand-mass first, gripped object second, finger separation last.
  Source: https://www.animeoutline.com/draw-anime-hands-holding-something-step-by-step/
- The index finger is frequently drawn separately from the other three when
  it needs a distinct angle (e.g. pointing slightly away or wrapping at a
  different angle than the rest), while the remaining fingers can stay as one
  combined mass initially and only get separated afterward if needed.
  Source: https://www.animeoutline.com/draw-anime-hands-holding-something-step-by-step/
- Fingers that wrap a cylindrical grip are built from overlapping "C"-shaped
  curved strokes, one such C per wrapping finger, stacked down the hilt.
  Source: search summary of https://drawing.wonderhowto.com/how-to/draw-hand-holding-sword-288285/ and https://idrawgirls.blogspot.com/2011/04/how-to-draw-hand-holding-sword.html
- Recommended technique for occlusion: draw the whole hand "see-through"
  (i.e. draw the fingers as if the pole were transparent) to get placement
  and spacing right, then erase/trim the parts hidden behind the gripped
  object. Source: https://www.animeoutline.com/draw-anime-hands-holding-something-step-by-step/
- Thumb in a grip acts as the opposing anchor, wrapping from the near side
  toward (or over) the fingers to close the grip; it is placed and drawn after
  the base of the thumb is established. Source: https://www.animeoutline.com/draw-anime-hands-holding-something-step-by-step/
- For a katana-style wrapped hilt specifically, the surface pattern (rhombus
  wrap segments with small fold lines) is separate from the hand/finger
  drawing itself, i.e. it decorates the pole, not the hand. Source: search
  summary of https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-a-katana-step-by-step/
- No source gives an exact count of visible finger segments/knuckle bumps for
  a simplified/chibi grip specifically. Inference (mine, not sourced): at
  small sizes a grip reads with as few as 2-3 stacked knuckle bumps along the
  side of the pole plus one thumb shape overlapping from the near side; this
  is an extrapolation from the general "stacked C-shapes" full-detail
  technique scaled down, not a stated simplification rule.

## 5. Line conventions

- Palm gets two interior creases in a fuller-detail treatment: one curving
  from the base of the thumb, another as a "wave" line below the base of the
  fingers (the base knuckle line). Source: https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-and-manga-hands/
- Each finger gets up to three creases at full detail: at its base, and at
  the second and third joints; the thumb gets one crease, at its one joint
  (third joint model for the thumb). Source: https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-and-manga-hands/
- Explicit statement that not all of this detail is always needed: hands on
  background or non-focal characters can drop fingernails and most/all
  interior creasing, keeping just the outer silhouette of the major masses
  (palm block, finger mass, thumb). Source: https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-and-manga-hands/
- Knuckle joints are described as being placed relative to where the
  construction ovals/segments meet, i.e. as interior lines at the joins
  between segments, not as notches cut into the outer silhouette; misplacing
  them is flagged as a specific error. Source: search summary of hand-anatomy
  tutorials, e.g. https://anatomy4sculptors.com/blog/hand-anatomy-for-artists/ and https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/11062
- On the "bunch of bananas" / claw problem specifically: no source used that
  exact phrase, but the closest documented causes are (a) drawing all three
  finger segments as equally distinct hard-edged sections rather than varying
  segment length and softening the tapers, which reads as unnatural/sausage-
  like, and (b) getting knuckle placement wrong so the joints don't line up
  with where the segments visually bend. Source: https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/11062
  [Connecting these causes to the "bananas"/"claws" descriptions is my own
  interpretation; no single source names those metaphors directly.]
- General method across sources for keeping lines under control: build the
  whole finger mass as one mitten shape first, and only cut in as many
  separation/crease lines as the detail level calls for, rather than drawing
  each finger as an independent stick from the start (which is what produces
  the "bananas fanned out" look). Sources: https://www.animeoutline.com/draw-anime-hands-holding-something-step-by-step/ , https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4890

## 6. Small-size legibility

- Explicit simplification-for-distance rule: fingernails and most interior
  creasing are the first details to drop; the outline of the major hand
  masses (palm block, four-finger mass, thumb) is kept as the minimum that
  still reads as a hand. Source: https://www.animeoutline.com/how-to-draw-anime-and-manga-hands/
- Chibi styles as a class default to simplified finger shapes and minimal
  detail generally (small hands, few or no separated fingers), which is
  effectively a permanent "small-size" ruleset rather than a distance-based
  one. Source: https://www.clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/155423
- No source gives a numeric px/size threshold for dropping specific lines
  (e.g. "below N px drop the knuckle crease"); the guidance found is
  qualitative (background/distant characters vs. focal characters). Applying
  this to a fixed head-radius-based figure (e.g. keep thumb-separation line
  always, add one knuckle-crease line only at >~2x head-radius scale, add
  finger-separation lines only at >~3x) is my own extrapolation for a
  codeable rule, not sourced.

## Recommended detail levels (my synthesis for this project, not a source's rule)

- At ~180px figure (head r ~21px, hand maybe ~10-12px long): keep the current
  mitten + thumb bump for the relaxed hand, but consider one short line
  separating the thumb from the palm mass (a crease, not a full outline cut)
  so it doesn't read as a paddle; no finger-separation lines are worth adding
  at this scale per the "drop creases first" rule above.
- At ~180px for the grip: mitten mass wrapped around the pole with 2 stacked
  knuckle-bump notches on the outline (not interior lines) plus the thumb
  bump overlapping from the near side; this matches the "outline of major
  masses only" small-size rule.
- At 3x scale (~540px figure, head r ~63px): relaxed hand can add one
  index-finger separation line plus a base-knuckle wave crease and a single
  thumb-base crease, consistent with the "fuller detail for focal-distance"
  guidance, still short of full 5-finger separation (which no source
  associates with chibi proportions at any distance).
- At 3x scale for the grip: add the second/third stacked "C" curve per
  wrapping finger (2-3 total) as interior lines along the pole-side of the
  hand, plus a thumb crease, following the full-detail "C"-shape stacking
  technique, still without separating fingers into independent silhouettes.
